The 2023 Kahramanmaraş doublet (Mw 7.8 followed by Mw 7.5) produced near-fault velocity pulses whose properties and orientations varied far more than a rupture model alone would predict. This study compares what the stations recorded against what a 3D dynamic rupture simulation reproduces, and asks what the difference is made of.

#### ***Scope & Methods***

1. Empirical pulse identification across the near-fault station network for both events of the doublet
2. 3D dynamic rupture simulation of the sequence, for comparison against the recordings
3. Assessment of pulse properties and orientations against directivity as the sole explanation

#### ***Key Findings***

1. Pulse properties and orientations vary substantially across the near-fault stations
2. The dynamic rupture simulation does not fully reproduce that variability
3. Directivity alone is insufficient to explain the observed pulse characteristics

#### ***Core Conclusion***

Local slip heterogeneities, fault geometry, and site effects — basin effects among them — are as critical as directivity in shaping near-fault pulses. Treating directivity as the single controlling factor understates what a site can experience.
